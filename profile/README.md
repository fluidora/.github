# Fluidora

**A management platform you compose, one module at a time.**

Management suites are sold as one block: you deploy everything, use a part of it, and every
team keeps its own spreadsheet on the side. Fluidora is sold by the module. A module is what
you buy, what one team owns, and what can be switched on in an afternoon without touching
anything else. Every module shares one account, one directory and one history.

## What we hold to

- **A module stands on its own.** Missing information from another module makes it degrade,
  not fail. Project without Capacity counts everyone at 100%.
- **One vocabulary, everywhere.** A term defined in the glossary is the term used in the docs,
  the code, the API and the interface.
- **The software reports, the human decides.** An overload is reported, never blocked.
- **Weeks, not days. Rates, not hours.** The ISO week is the unit of everything.
- **The interface gets out of the way.** One primary action per view, defaults that work.

## AI-native and terminal-first

Whatever you can do in Fluidora, an agent acting for you can do too. The API is the product,
and every interface is a client of it:

- **the web interface**, for people who work in a browser;
- **a CLI**, for people who work in a terminal, with clean, coloured output and tables;
- **an MCP server**, so your assistant can discover and call the same capabilities.

All of them act in your account, with your rights, and see nothing you would not see. AI
proposes; a person decides, and the history keeps who did.

## Where we are

The Project module is built and the foundation is specified. A beta opens to a limited number
of teams; the MCP server, the CLI and AI-assisted selection come after it.
